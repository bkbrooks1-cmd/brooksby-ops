# The archetype layer

**Version:** v1.0 | 2026-08-28
**What this is:** the ⬡ archetype laws that survived three register runs on the reference build, packaged to seed a new subject's `voice.md`. This is the layer that ships. **No instance data from the reference subject's profile appears here** — none of their proof points, tics, audience, phrase bank, or client references. The footer attributes the method, which is not the same thing.

**How to use it.** Seed a new profile from the confirmed laws below, then **verify each one against that subject's own clean samples before keeping it.** A seeded law is a hypothesis. Delete the ones that do not fire. Every deletion is a finding worth logging: it means the law was instance wearing an archetype mark.

**Every entry carries its evidence.** Observed in / checked against / scope, per the scoping gate. Read the scope before applying the law — three of the entries below are scoped to one kind of document and will misfire elsewhere. That is not caution, it is the specific failure this file exists to prevent: on the reference build two laws shipped with the wrong scope in two days, both derived from a single register, both describing that register rather than the writer.

**Evidence base.** Three clean dictated samples across three registers, spanning 2026-08-18 to 2026-08-26: a short public post (~8 min dictated), a long-form argument (2,410 words), and a client-facing system description (~1,700 words). Plus two real documents used as validation, one written nineteen days before the laws existed. One subject. **Every ⬡ mark below is a bet that a finding from one person generalizes.**

## Tier 1 — Confirmed across three registers

These fired, or were correctly absent for a stated reason, in every register tested. Start here.

### ⬡ Guarantee by limitation

**Do:** when describing a system, a service, a method, or a scope of work, state what it will **not** do in the same breath as what it does, and let the refusal carry the credibility.
**Avoid:** a capabilities list with the limits collected in a caveats section at the end. That reads as disclaiming. The limit belongs next to the claim it bounds.

> "It never sends anything out that is not approved to go out."
> "Nothing runs when the app is closed."
> "Nothing ever ships without a full human review."

**The trigger is the job, not the document type.** Wherever a piece has to make something *believable* rather than merely argue a position, the refusals show up.

⚠ **This law sits directly on top of the negation ban and must not be allowed to collide with it.** Both govern sentences containing "not," which is exactly the overlap that makes a file produce a banned construction while appearing compliant.

- **Allowed — the limit follows the thing.** "It is a snapshot, not a live page."
- **Banned — the negation defines the thing.** "It isn't a live page. It's a snapshot."

**Test: delete the negative clause.** If what remains is a true standalone statement, it is a guarantee. If nothing remains, it was a definition by negation.

*Observed in:* client system description, 7 instances. *Checked against:* a case study written 19 days before the law existed (4 instances) and a published long-form argument (2 instances). *Scope:* **general.** Three independent appearances across three registers, two of them predating the law. This is the most robust finding on the reference build and the first piece that was ever shippable.

### ⬡ Closings follow the piece's intent. There is no default close.

Ask what the piece is *for*, then pick the ending.

- Making an argument → end on the work. Short imperative fragments.
- Selling something → end on the pitch. That is a legitimate intent, not a failure of restraint.
- Telling a story → end on the thing that happened. No moral, no lesson stated aloud.
- Opening a real question → end on the question, when the writer genuinely does not know and wants replies from people who might.

**How to tell an earned closing question from a reflex.** The banned close is a question the writer already knows the answer to, appended to a piece that resolved. The allowed close is a question the piece has spent its length failing to resolve. **Test: if the piece contains a sentence admitting the writer has no good answer, the question at the end is earned.** Ask no more than two.

*Observed in:* draft-reaction, session 1 — the subject rejected the reflexive closing question he had published nine times out of nine. *Checked against:* all three registers, which closed three different ways (pitch / questions / offer) and were correct each time. *Scope:* **general.**

### ⬡ When a story lands, stop

Do not restate a story's point after telling it — not as a moral, not as a summary, not as an equation. End the story, then go straight to its consequence. **No bridge between the two.**

The restatement explains the point to a reader who has just understood it, and it buries the short sentence doing the real work.

*Observed in:* long-form argument — cutting a three-sentence arithmetic restatement after a fable was the single highest-value edit of that pass. *Checked against:* survived the subject's own publish pass a week later, and he then applied the same rule unprompted to a second passage nobody had flagged. Independently present in a channel-register file tuned months earlier from publish edits. *Scope:* **general.** Two methods sharing no inputs.

### ⬡ Lead with what a thing is

Never define something by first saying what it isn't. The two-sentence version is the same move wearing a period, and splitting it does not launder it.

**Bad:** "A diagnostic isn't a deliverable. It's a decision input."
**Good:** "A diagnostic is the key input to your decision making."

**The one exception is correction.** If the reader actually holds the belief being negated, reject it first and then supply the replacement — you are correcting them, not introducing yourself. **Test before using it:** is this a belief the reader already has, or am I inventing a foil to knock down? If the second, cut it and state the claim.

*Observed in:* draft-reaction, session 1. *Checked against:* the carve-out fired correctly on a real sentence in the long-form argument, and the delete-the-negative-clause test correctly permitted a real sentence in the validation document that a blunt reading would have cut. *Scope:* **general.**

### ⬡ Name the actual tool, every time

Name the product. Not "project management tools," not "the CRM," not "AI tools" when a specific thing is meant.

**Override:** where anonymization applies, the anonymization outranks this law. A validation document deliberately named no vendor and no platform, and that was correct.

*Observed in:* clean corpus and all three registers. *Checked against:* 3/3, plus one correct deliberate suppression. *Scope:* **general, with the anonymization override.**

### ⬡ Do the homework, then show it

Name the actual figure, the actual class, the actual year. The specificity *is* the credibility. **Do not thin the density out to keep a piece short.**

*Observed in:* clean corpus, where the subject named the piece he considered truest as the one with the most researched detail in it. *Checked against:* dense in both long-form registers; correctly sparse in the descriptive register, where there was nothing to measure. *Scope:* **general.**

### ⬡ Rule of three is dead as a default

Use the number of items the content actually has. Two is fine. Four is fine. One is fine. **Never manufacture a beat to reach three, and never cut a real one to get down to three.**

*Observed in:* draft-reaction, session 1 — three-item lists were one of four habits the subject rejected on sight after publishing them repeatedly. *Checked against:* the validation document's one three-item list had three distinct load-bearing items, not a padded third. *Scope:* **general.** This is an AI-artifact ban, which is the basis for the archetype mark.

### ⬡ Warm about people, cold about process

Never blame a team or a named individual. The failure is structural, every time. The writer's own failures are the exception and get told flat.

*Observed in:* clean corpus. *Checked against:* the validation document describes an opposing party's incentive as structural asymmetry rather than bad faith. Independently present in a channel-register file as a form principle. *Scope:* **general.** Two methods sharing no inputs.

## Tier 2 — Confirmed, scoped

These are real and they misfire outside their scope. **Apply the scope; do not generalize them.**

### ⬡ Argue by concession — argument-side registers

**Do:** after the strongest version of a claim, immediately name what it does *not* mean, and give the number or the limit that proves you know the difference.
**Avoid:** defending the maximal version, or saving the caveats for a closing paragraph.

The concession is not hedging. It comes right after the assertion, in its own sentences, and it is what makes the assertion survivable. **Never soften the original claim to avoid needing the concession**, and never manufacture one — it follows a claim strong enough to need it.

**Where trust has to be established rather than argued, this converts rather than disappearing.** Zero concessions in ~1,700 words of client-facing description, against four in 2,400 words of argument. What replaced it was guarantee by limitation, above. Same reflex, different object.

*Observed in:* long-form argument, 4 instances. *Checked against:* validation document, 3 instances; **absent** from the client register. Independently present in a channel-register file as "steel-man and counterweight." *Scope:* **argument-side registers. Converts to guarantee by limitation elsewhere.**

### ⬡ Number first, meaning second — argument and post registers only

Lead the section with the measurement, then say what it means. Avoid a setup paragraph explaining why the number matters before giving it.

**Where it does not apply:** a descriptive or procedural document may have nothing to lead a section with. **Do not manufacture a figure to satisfy this law.** That is the inverse of "never invent a specific" and it is worse, because a fabricated number in a client document is a defect the client can act on.

*Observed in:* long-form argument, every evidence section. *Checked against:* validation document, partial (2 of 11 sections); **absent** from the client register, whose only numbers were inventory counts. Independently present in a channel-register file as "receipts before rhetoric." *Scope:* **argument and post registers.** This law shipped unqualified on the reference build and was live and wrong for seven days. It is the reason the scoping gate exists.

### ⬡ Describe a system through the command that triggers it — system-description documents

**Do:** quote the actual command or trigger the reader would type, then say what follows.
**Avoid:** describing a capability in the abstract when there is a phrase the user would actually use.

> "When you say 'capture,' each of those call notes becomes a meeting page…"

It puts the reader's own action first and the machinery second, which is why the document reads as something they operate rather than something that happens to them.

*Observed in:* client system description, 6 instances. *Checked against:* absent from both other registers — **no occasion**, since neither describes an operable system. *Scope:* **system-description documents.** Absence-without-occasion is weaker evidence than absence-with-occasion; treat the scope as unproven at the edges.

## Tier 3 — Provisional. Thin evidence, no counter-evidence.

**These are the first candidates to be wrong.** Seed them only if the subject's register map includes the matching register, and check them hard. Each entry says exactly how thin its evidence is; one of them has none at all and is kept as a worked example.

### ⬡ PROVISIONAL — Anticipatory reassurance

When a register strips a writer's warmth markers, look for what the warmth converted into rather than concluding it left. In a client-facing document the substitute observed was **answering the reader's unasked worry, in passing, before they raise it.**

> "You'll find that you reference it very little."
> "You read and send your email like you always do."

**A flat client document is not a voice failure. A client document with no reassurance in it is.**

⚠ **This reads as hedging and a polish pass will strip it.** Carve it out explicitly: a hedge weakens a claim the *writer* is making; reassurance answers a worry the *reader* is having. Do not distribute it — two instances carried a document of roughly 1,700 words.

*Observed in:* client system description, 2 instances. *Checked against:* the validation case study, where it fired on a live sentence; **absent** from the other two registers. *Scope:* **two documents, one register. Provisional** — stronger than a single observation, not yet general.

### ⬡ PROVISIONAL — Give the mechanism, not just the outcome

Explain how the thing happens. Do not assert that it happens and move to the close.

*Observed in:* clean corpus, pre-register. *Checked against:* **not checked.** No register run tracked it and none of the three finding tables lists it. *Scope:* **unproven.**

⚠ *This entry is here as a worked example of the gate doing its job. It was drafted into Tier 1 claiming general scope and "present in all three registers," and the verification pass found no register run had ever recorded it. The claim was written from memory of the law's plausibility, not from evidence — which is the exact mechanism behind both wrong-scoped laws on the reference build. It stays in the file, demoted, rather than being quietly deleted.*

### ⬡ PROVISIONAL — Credit a borrowed frame, then restate it in your own terms

Name the person, link the piece, then convert it to the writer's own situation in the next breath. Never let someone else's framework carry a paragraph alone, and never use a citation as a substitute for having a position.

*Observed in:* long-form argument, 2 instances. *Checked against:* **no occasion** in the other two registers — nothing was borrowed in either. *Scope:* **provisional.**

## ⬡ Bans that hold regardless of subject

*Provenance: draft-reaction, session 1, plus the clean-corpus audit. **Scope: general, on a stated basis** — each is marked archetype because the construction it bans is one an assistant produces by default, not because one subject dislikes it. That basis is the whole justification for the mark; if a ban below turns out to rest only on the subject's taste, it is ◆ and should be moved.*

They came out of draft-reaction on one subject and are kept on the artifact grounds above.

- **Never open with a rhetorical question.**
- **Never let formatting do the work an argument should** — one-line paragraphs stacked for cadence, emoji bullets, a hook that is all setup. If structure is carrying a piece with no argument underneath, the piece is not ready.
- **Never hedge a claim the subject would state flat.**
- **Never invent a specific.** If a draft needs a number, a detail, a date, or a tool the subject did not supply, flag the gap and leave it. **A gap they can see is fixable. A gap you filled is a defect they cannot see.**
- **Never claim an experience the subject has not had.**
- **Never make an identifiable individual the reason something broke** — including people who would not recognize themselves.
- **Never write in a spelling convention other than the subject's.** This holds against any invoked skill's convention, and gets re-checked after every structure or humanizer pass, because that is where the drift comes from.
- **Avoid the two-clause reversal aphorism** ("Tools don't fix processes. They ratify them."). Zero instances across six clean samples spanning twelve years on the reference build. It is a content-pipeline artifact that gets attributed back to the writer as a signature move. **Do not reach for it, and do not let it sit in a phrase bank while a law bans it forty lines away** — that exact collision was found and removed.

## ⬡ The anti-overfitting guard — seed this into every profile

*Provenance: accumulated across all three register runs. Every line below was added because a rule already in the file was over-applied to a real draft, or because a rate was mistaken for a pattern. **Scope: general** — the failure it prevents is a property of the model, not of the subject.*

Do not imitate the profile so hard that the writing becomes a performance of it. Adapt the specifics to the subject; keep the shape.

- Do not make every sentence short if the corpus has long ones.
- Do not deploy the subject's signature device in every section. Once per piece. Twice is a tic.
- Do not open every piece the same way. Different intents open differently.
- Do not add enthusiasm markers where the enthusiasm is not real.
- Do not avoid a word that is the exact right word because it appears on a banlist.
- Do not manufacture a concession. It follows a claim strong enough to need one.
- Do not manufacture a refusal. Guarantee by limitation works because the limits are real and load-bearing. Inventing a thing a system "will never do" in order to sound like the subject is the same defect as inventing a number.
- Do not end every section the same way — note the *observed rate*, not the pattern. Five of eight is an instruction; eight of eight is a performance.
- Do not distribute a device that appeared twice in a long document.

Write it straight first. Then remove what sounds machine-made.

**The test:** does this sound like something the subject would actually write, or like a model trying hard to imitate them?

## ⬡ The contrarian-essay archetype — for a subject who publishes a newsletter

*Provenance: a channel-register file tuned across five published issues, reconciled once against a verified-clean long-form dictation. **Scope: this channel only** — it governs one publishing channel and must never be applied to the subject's other registers. Three of its rules (steel-man, receipts-before-rhetoric, ending on a question) were independently confirmed by the dictation, which is the convergence test; they are load-bearing and should not be softened. Four others were found over-fit to two published issues and were widened.*

**Lineage.** This is the ⬡ layer of a channel-register file that has been *tuned* across published issues, not generated. It is the strongest archetype in this package because it was battle-tested by real publish edits before it was ever generalized.

⚠ **This is a dated extract, not a pointer.** The canonical file lives in the reference build's private folder and does not ship. **Re-extract at each plugin release**, and never sync in the other direction — a client's tuning belongs in the client's own file.

**Voice principles.**

1. Every issue takes one position and defends it. No fence-sitting, no "it depends" as a conclusion.
2. Receipts before rhetoric. The argument stands on counted numbers from the writer's own practice, cited sources, or named public history. If neither exists, the claim gets cut.
3. Warm to people, hard on broken processes. Clients, teams, and prospects are never the villain; the sequence, the stack, or the process is.
4. Publish the unflattering numbers. A ledger shown only when it flatters is not a ledger.
5. Leave real threads open. Unresolved problems stay unresolved on the page and get revisited later.

**Opening.** Name the consensus, counter it, put receipts on the table. Source the consensus personally, not abstractly — "I hear from folks that…" beats "Everyone says…". Credibility arrives through specifics, never through titles. 60 to 120 words before the first break.

**Section flow — a default shape, not a checklist.** Five to eight sections, sentence-case headers. Sections may be dropped, merged, or reordered when the argument requires it. **Never manufacture a section to complete the sequence.**

1. The consensus and the counter · 2. Who this hits *(droppable when the evidence section does that work)* · 3. The evidence · 4. The turn — what the evidence showed that the writer did not expect · 5. Steel-man and counterweight, plus what the position does not cover · 6. Implication — what the reader does differently Monday · 7. Closer, 30 to 60 words, never a summary.

**Evidence.** Every load-bearing claim carries a number, counted rather than estimated — and say which if estimated. Opposing data appears honestly. Example-to-abstraction ratio roughly 3:1. Citations link inline; never invoke unnamed studies or experts. **One data table permitted, and only when the table *is* the evidence** — a measured comparison prose would blur. Prose still carries the argument around it, and the paragraph after the table says what it means. Label both columns when one side is estimated and the other counted.

**Formatting.** Prose carries the argument. No lists in narrative sections; numbered lists only when order matters. Bold and italic at most once each per issue. No emojis, no pull-quote decoration.

**Closing.** A reply invitation aimed at the reader's own operation. No summary, no motivational wrap, no hard pitch. **Ask at most twice** — once in the closer, at most once earlier where a specific question genuinely arises. Repetition reads as needing the reply rather than wanting it.

**What this form never does.** Straw-mans the other side — the strongest objection gets told as a real story. Hedges the position into mush. Blames a person when a process is the problem. Sells; the strongest issues pitch nothing and end on a question. Wraps with a neat moral or restates itself. Performs honesty ("to be honest," "frankly") instead of just being direct. Uses the "It isn't X, it's Y" reframe. Stages a scene when the literal detail exists. Trims the tool list to a shorthand version.

**Length.** Standard issues 1,000 to 1,600 words; an argued case-study issue with a ledger may run longer. Length follows the evidence. **Never cut a counted number to hit a ceiling** — and watch the ceiling itself. On the reference build it was raised once and then exceeded once. A third upward move means it is not a ceiling.

**◆ Everything not listed above is instance and does not ship:** the audience definition, the writer's proof points, their signoff, their anonymization conventions, and their publish-edit history.
